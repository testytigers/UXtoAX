Your UX skills aren't obsolete. They're the foundation for what comes next. But making the move from UX to AX isn't just about adding AI to your toolkit — it's about expanding your mental model of what interaction design is.

Here's how to do it deliberately.

## Start with understanding, not tools

The worst transition mistake is jumping straight to tools and prompts without building a mental model of how AI agents work. Spend time with the fundamentals first.

You don't need a computer science degree. You need to understand:

- **What language models are good at and bad at.** They're good at synthesis, pattern completion, and flexible communication. They're bad at precise counting, reliable source citation, and consistent behaviour across runs.
- **How agents differ from chat.** Agents can take actions — browse the web, run code, call APIs, write and execute plans. That changes the design problem dramatically.
- **Why agents fail.** Hallucination, context window limits, tool call failures, ambiguous instructions. Each is a design surface.

Spend two weeks using Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini not as users but as designers. Notice where trust breaks. Notice where you lose the thread of what the agent is doing. Notice what you wish it had told you. Those are AX problems.

## Audit your existing skills

Most UX practitioners are better positioned for AX than they realise. The gap is narrower than it looks:

**Conversation design** is in your job description now, whether or not it's listed. Every prompt you craft, every clarification flow you design, every error message for an AI action — that's conversation design. If you've done content design or UX writing, you have a head start.

**Research methods** transfer almost entirely. You still recruit users, observe behaviour, and synthesise findings. The behaviour you're observing has changed — users delegating to agents rather than navigating interfaces — but the research craft is the same.

**Systems thinking** is more important in AX than in most UX work. Agents operate within systems: they have memory, access to tools, and relationships with other agents. Designing one agent means understanding how it behaves in context.

**Where you'll need to build:** failure state design at scale, trust pattern design, behavior specifications as a design artefact, and enough AI literacy to prototype directly with models.

## Build the portfolio before you need it

The fastest path to an AX role is a portfolio that shows you've already done the thinking. Recruiters and hiring managers for AX roles are still figuring out what "good" looks like — which means a rigorous, well-documented case study will stand out dramatically.

Three project types that work well:

**Redesign as agent.** Take a workflow you know well — onboarding, search, support — and design it as an agent-mediated experience. What does the agent need to know? What does it do if the user's intent is ambiguous? What's the failure experience?

**Failure audit.** Use an AI product you know and document every failure you encounter over a week. Then design the fixes. This shows research skills, AX-specific thinking, and practical familiarity with how agents break.

**Trust pattern library.** Design a set of components for communicating agent confidence, showing source attribution, and giving users control. This is the AX equivalent of a design system contribution.

## The job titles to watch

Don't only search for "AX Designer." Look for:

- AI UX Designer
- Human-AI Interaction Designer
- Conversational Experience Designer
- AI Product Designer
- Responsible AI Designer

The discipline is being named in real time. Companies are using different words for similar roles. Cast a wide net, read the job descriptions carefully, and look for the signal words: agents, LLMs, trust, transparency, conversation flows.

## The honest timeline

If you commit to 5–10 hours a week:

- **Month 1–2:** Build AI literacy, start using models as a designer, map your skill gaps.
- **Month 3–4:** Complete two portfolio projects. Document them thoroughly.
- **Month 5–6:** Start applying. Use your network. Talk to people already in the field.
- **Month 6–12:** Expect rejections, iteration on your portfolio, and eventually traction.

The market for AX designers is early and growing fast. The designers who make the move now — thoughtfully, rigorously, with real portfolio work — will have an enormous advantage over those who wait until the field is saturated.

The shift is real. The roles are being created now. Your UX skills are the foundation. The rest is deliberate practice.
