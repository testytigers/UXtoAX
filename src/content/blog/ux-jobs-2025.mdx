The headlines alternate between extremes. "AI is killing UX design" and "UX designers are more important than ever" can coexist because the truth is more specific than either headline suggests.

The UX job market is restructuring, not collapsing. Understanding where growth is happening is more useful than debating the overall trend.

## Roles under pressure

**Visual/UI-only roles** — The gap between UI design and AI-generated UI has closed significantly. Roles that are primarily about producing polished screens with minimal systems thinking are the most exposed.

**Junior generalist UX** — Entry-level generalist roles have contracted at larger companies. The bar for what "junior" means has risen because more of the producible work can be automated.

**Research operations** — Survey design, transcript processing, and participant recruitment are increasingly automated. Research roles that don't involve synthesis, strategy, and judgment are shrinking.

## Roles growing

**AI product design** — Teams building AI-native products need designers who understand agent behavior, not just UI patterns. These roles exist at every stage of company growth.

**Conversation design** — Every company building a voice or chat interface needs conversation designers. The supply of qualified candidates is still well below demand.

**Agent experience design** — A small but rapidly growing category. Companies deploying internal AI agents for workflows need someone to own the interaction model. That person is usually a UX designer who learned the domain.

**Trust and safety design** — Designing for appropriate use, harm mitigation, and responsible AI is a function that every AI-forward company needs and few have staffed adequately.

**Research for AI** — Understanding how users relate to AI systems — their mental models, trust calibration, delegation behavior — is a research specialty in genuine demand.

## The emerging role that doesn't have a name yet

The most interesting emerging role sits at the intersection of product design, behavior specification, and AI system design. It doesn't have a consistent job title yet — you'll see it as "AI Product Designer," "Agent Designer," "Interaction Designer (AI)," and variations.

What it requires: UX foundations, systems thinking, working knowledge of how LLMs and agents function, and the ability to translate behavioral requirements into something engineers can build.

That's the role to prepare for.
