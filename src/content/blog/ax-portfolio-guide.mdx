The AX design market is early. Most hiring managers are figuring out what they want at the same time they're interviewing candidates. That's an advantage if you understand how to use it.

Your portfolio isn't being compared against an established standard. You're helping define what good looks like.

## What to show

Classic UX portfolios show screens. AX portfolios need to show thinking.

The deliverables are different: behavior specifications, agent state diagrams, conversation flow maps, failure mode analyses, trust calibration frameworks. If you're still presenting these as wireframes, you're speaking the wrong language.

**What a strong AX case study contains:**

- A clear problem statement that involves autonomous agent behavior
- A user research component that addresses mental models, trust, or delegation behavior
- An agent design artifact — behavior spec, decision tree, tool access diagram, or similar
- Explicit treatment of failure states and edge cases
- A reflection on what you'd do differently

## How to frame existing UX work

If you don't have agentic projects yet, you can re-frame existing work through an AX lens. Take a product you worked on and ask: what would it look like if an agent could accomplish the same outcome autonomously? What would it need to perceive? What decisions would it need to make? Where would it need human intervention?

Showing this analysis — even as a hypothetical extension of existing work — demonstrates the thinking shift that AX roles require.

## What not to show

Screenshots of prompting ChatGPT are not design work. UI mockups of chatbot interfaces without behavior documentation are incomplete. Figma prototypes of voice assistants without conversation design documentation are decorative.

The AX hiring bar is moving. Right now it's low, but it's moving fast. The portfolios that will look thin in 18 months are the ones that show only surfaces without systems.

## Positioning your narrative

You're not trying to look like an AI engineer who wandered into design. You're a designer who understands AI deeply enough to define how it should behave. That's a rare combination and the market is paying for it.

Lead with your design sensibility. Frame your AI knowledge as what makes your design decisions better, not as a separate credential.
