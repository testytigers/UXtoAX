Language is the oldest interface. Conversation design is the practice of making AI systems speak it well.

This is not about making chatbots sound friendly. It's about designing dialogue structures that serve user goals, handle unexpected inputs, and recover gracefully from misunderstanding — at scale, across the full range of user intents.

## The dialogue structure

Every conversation has structure, even when it feels free-form. Conversation designers make that structure explicit and deliberate.

**Opening** — How does the conversation begin? What expectations does the system set? What does the user learn about what's possible in the first exchange?

**Intent resolution** — How does the system identify what the user wants? What happens when the intent is unclear, compound, or outside the system's scope?

**Turn management** — When does the system ask a clarifying question versus proceed with its best interpretation? Too many questions frustrate users; too few lead to wrong outputs.

**Context tracking** — What does the system remember within a conversation? What can users reference from earlier in the exchange, and what falls out of scope?

**Closing** — How does the conversation end? Is the user confident the task is complete? What do they do next?

## Writing for AI voice

The language an AI system uses shapes how users model it. Formal language creates authority-seeking behavior. Casual language creates over-familiarity. Neither is inherently wrong — but both need to be deliberate.

Principles for AI language that works:

- **Be specific about uncertainty** — "I'm not sure" is less useful than "I don't have access to your account history to answer that"
- **Avoid false confidence** — hedging that's calibrated to actual uncertainty builds trust; hedging on everything destroys it
- **Match user register** — professional tools should sound professional, consumer products can be warmer
- **Surface alternatives** — when the system can't do what was asked, offer what it can do

## The edge case is the rule

Traditional UI design treats edge cases as special handling. In conversation design, the edge case is half your work. Users will say things you didn't anticipate, combine intents in unexpected ways, and speak in dialects and idioms your system wasn't trained on.

Your conversation design isn't finished when the happy path works. It's finished when the unhappy paths are as deliberately designed as the ones you expected.
